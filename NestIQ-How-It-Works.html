01 · Introduction

One desk for the whole portfolio.

NestIQ is a property management platform for South African landlords. It keeps properties, people, rent, leases, and maintenance in one place, and gives each person only the view they need.

A landlord signs in and sees the portfolio: which units are occupied, who has paid, which leases are ending, and which repairs are still open. A tenant signs in and sees their own home: rent status, the lease, documents, messages, and a way to report a problem. A caretaker sees the properties they have been assigned. A contractor sees the jobs sent to them. A NestIQ administrator sees the platform itself — accounts, billing, support, and announcements.

Nothing has to be installed from an app store. NestIQ runs in the browser on a phone or a computer. The interface can be read in English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, isiXhosa, and Sesotho, and it can be switched between a light theme, a dark theme, and several colour themes.

Every person around a property gets a door of their own. The landlord keeps the keys to the building.

02 · Roles

Five doors, one building.

NestIQ is organised by role. Signing in as the wrong kind of person does not open the wrong rooms. A tenant cannot wander into the landlord’s books, and a contractor cannot see a job that was never assigned to them.

Landlord

The owner or manager of the portfolio. Creates the account, adds properties and units, publishes vacancies, approves applications, sets leases, tracks rent, invites staff, and reads the financial reports.

Tenant

Starts as an applicant. Creates a free account, browses listings, and applies for a vacant unit. After approval, the same account becomes their home portal: lease, payments, maintenance, documents, and messages.

Caretaker

On-site staff invited by the landlord with a code. Works only on assigned properties: tenants at those properties, maintenance, inspections, and messages with the landlord.

Contractor

A vendor who has been given a portal login. Sees the jobs assigned to them, updates the work, writes what was done, and records an invoice total when the job is finished.

Administrator

Runs NestIQ as a product. Sees landlords, tenants, and staff across the platform, subscription billing, maintenance volume, support tickets, the changelog, announcements, and accounts that need a closer look. This is a NestIQ operator, separate from a landlord’s own team.

03 · The core journey

From a vacant unit to a signed lease.

Tenants are not typed in by hand. A vacancy is published, a person applies, and the landlord decides. That single path keeps the record clean from the first click.

1

List

The landlord marks a unit vacant. It appears in Available listings with rent, type, and location.

2

Apply

A prospective tenant searches by suburb or city, saves favourites, and submits an application from their account.

3

Decide

The landlord reviews the application, the documents, and — if they choose — an AI screening note, then approves or declines.

4

Occupy

Approval creates the tenant profile. They sign the lease in the portal and the unit shows as occupied.

What happens after the keys

The lease carries the monthly rent, the deposit, the start and end dates, and the day rent falls due. NestIQ then keeps the month moving on its own rhythm.

  • The rent calendar marks what is due and what is overdue for each tenant.
  • Reminders can go out by email or WhatsApp, and every call, message, letter, or visit can be logged against the tenant.
  • The tenant sees Paid, Rent due, or Overdue, together with the history of what has already been recorded.
  • A repair request raised in the portal lands on the landlord’s maintenance board, and can be handed to a caretaker or a contractor.
  • As the end date approaches, the lease appears in renewals and on the dashboard’s expiration list.
  • When someone is preparing to leave, the tenant portal can show a move-out checklist.
Banking details travel with the rent. During setup the landlord saves the bank name, account number, branch code, account type, and a payment reference such as tenant name, month, and year. Those details appear on reminders and receipts so the tenant knows how to pay the landlord directly. NestIQ keeps the ledger of what is due and what has been recorded. The in-app card and EFT steps confirm that record inside the product. Rent itself moves between the tenant and the landlord. NestIQ’s own subscription is a separate charge, billed through PayFast.

04 · Landlord

The morning the portfolio opens.

After email verification, a new landlord receives fourteen days of Pro at no charge, with no card required to begin. A short wizard asks for the profile, the first property, a reminder of how tenants join, and the banking details used on receipts.

The home screen

The signed-in home is a working desk, not a brochure. It greets the landlord by name and lays out the things that usually need a decision today.

At a glance

Portfolio stats, a grid of properties, open maintenance, recent transactions, a maintenance overview, a chart of rent collected through the year, and leases that are coming to an end.

Waiting on you

Badges mark unread tenant messages, new applications, and open maintenance. A bell collects notifications and communication follow-ups that are due.

Properties and units

Each property stores a name, address, city, type, bedrooms, rent, and status. Types cover apartments, houses, townhouses, bachelor apartments, and backrooms or garden flats. The list can be searched and filtered by occupied, vacant, rent due, or type, then switched between a card grid and a units view. Photos open in a lightbox. One building can mix occupied and vacant homes, and the vacant units are what public listings offer to applicants. Archived properties can be shown again when needed.

The rest of the landlord menu

People

Tenants holds profiles, messages, and the link back to each lease. Applications is the queue of people waiting for a yes or a no, with a count on the navigation badge. Vendors is the address book of trades, and the place a contractor invitation is sent.

Operations

Maintenance is the job board. Inspections records condition checks. The rent calendar places due dates, overdue amounts, and lease ends on one month view. Leases is where agreements are followed through to renewal.

Finance

Analytics, deposits, late fees, and the rent roll. The roll exports as a confidential PDF. Settings, billing, help, and the changelog live under Account.

05 · Tenant

A portal that fits in a pocket.

Tenants never download an app. They create a free NestIQ account, browse listings, and — once approved — sign in to the same account and find their home already waiting.

Before they move in

Available listings can be searched by suburb or city, sorted by newest or by price, and filtered by property type. A heart saves a favourite. Applying uses the account they already created, and the decision is tracked under Applications in My Portal, with a badge when something has changed.

After they are approved

The portal opens with a greeting and a home view of the tenancy: the property, the unit, rent status, the lease card, and the next things that need attention. The side navigation stays the same on a phone or a desktop.

Payments

Paid, due, or overdue, with the amount, the history, a small chart, and any late fees. Pay rent opens a short confirmation so the ledger can be updated.

Maintenance

A leaking tap, a faulty geyser, a broken lock — submitted here, with status the tenant can follow from open to resolved.

Documents

The lease and anything else the landlord has shared. New files are badged until the tenant has opened them.

Messages and notices

A direct thread with the landlord, plus notices posted to the tenancy. Unread items show as a count on the tab.

Profile and the home itself

Contact details, property information, utility readings the landlord has recorded, a mini calendar of dates that matter, and a move-out checklist when the lease is ending.

The tenant sees their own lease, their own balance, and their own repairs. The rest of the portfolio stays with the landlord.

Tenants can also open listings and the property map while signed in, so they can keep looking if they are between homes. They are kept out of landlord pages such as the rent roll, expenses, and other tenants’ records.

06 · Caretaker

The person on the property.

A caretaker is invited from a specific property. The invitation carries a code, a link, and the landlord’s name. They choose the staff portal, enter the code, and sign up with the email the invitation was sent to.

Once inside, the caretaker dashboard is deliberately smaller than the landlord’s. The work is the building in front of them.

Home

A welcome, the tenants at assigned properties, and the latest maintenance so the day starts with what is already in motion.

Properties

Only the properties the landlord assigned. If nothing has been assigned yet, the portal says so plainly.

Maintenance

Requests grouped into tabs such as new work and work in progress, with cancelled items available when they need to be seen. The caretaker moves jobs along for those properties.

Inspections

Condition checks for the assigned stock, so a move-in, a routine visit, or a move-out can be recorded without opening the landlord’s full books.

Messages sit beside the work, with a badge for anything unread. Profile shows the caretaker’s name, email, and how many properties they cover, and sign-out is always one tap away. If a caretaker’s account is suspended by a NestIQ administrator, the portal locks while the landlord’s staff record remains in place.

What stays with the landlord. Rent rolls, deposits, late fees, and tenants at other properties never appear in this portal. The caretaker works the building they were invited to, and the books stay on the landlord’s desk.

07 · Contractor

A job, a note, a finished visit.

Contractors begin as vendors. The landlord keeps a vendor list — plumbers, electricians, and the other trades — and invites the ones who should work inside NestIQ. The invitation is the same staff-code pattern used for caretakers, with the contractor role on it.

What the contractor sees

  • Home summarises active jobs so the next visit is obvious.
  • My Jobs lists work currently assigned. Opening a job shows the problem, the property, and room to record the work.
  • Completed keeps finished jobs, including what was done and the invoice total when one was entered.
  • Profile holds the contractor’s own details and sign-out.

A contractor does not see the rent roll, other vendors’ jobs, or tenants who have nothing to do with their visit. The landlord remains the person who assigns the work and closes the loop with the tenant.

On the job card

The description of the problem, the work carried out, completion notes, and an invoice total in rand. The landlord’s maintenance board picks up the same status, so the tenant’s “open” request and the contractor’s “done” visit are one record.

From the vendor list to a visit. The landlord keeps the trade on Vendors, sends the staff code, and assigns the request. The contractor updates that same request until it is resolved, and the tenant watches the status change in their own portal.

09 · Finance

Rent, deposits, and the books.

NestIQ is the ledger and the reminder system. It shows what should have arrived, what has been recorded, and what the portfolio is earning after costs.

The monthly rhythm

1Due date on the lease
2Calendar and dashboard
3Reminder by email or WhatsApp
4Payment recorded
5Receipt and rent roll

Each tenant has a monthly amount taken from the active lease. The rent roll lays the portfolio out as a sheet: property, unit, tenant, and the month. It can be printed or saved as a PDF marked confidential for the landlord. Analytics turns the same numbers into charts — revenue, occupancy, and the shape of the year.

When rent is late

Late Fee Management holds the landlord’s policy and a ledger of fees that are outstanding, paid, or waived. Fees can be applied across overdue accounts in one action, and a late-fee notice can be sent and logged with the rest of the communication history. The tenant sees those fees on their payments tab.

Deposits

Deposit Management follows the life of a security deposit: collection, interest, deductions, refunds, and claims, with the Rental Housing Act in mind. The page summarises compliance and can be searched by tenant or property. Whether a deposit has been collected is also visible from the lease, so a new tenancy is not left half-finished.

Expenses and tax

The expense tracker records costs against the portfolio. The tax summary turns income and those costs into a report a landlord can take to their own adviser at year end. NestIQ prepares the summary. It does not file a return.

Reconciliation, yield, value

Bank reconciliation lines the NestIQ ledger up with what the bank shows. The ROI calculator works out gross yield, net yield, cash flow, and return. Portfolio valuation keeps a view of what the buildings are worth alongside what they earn.

Two kinds of payment. Tenant rent is arranged with the landlord and recorded in NestIQ, using the banking details saved in setup. The landlord’s NestIQ subscription — Pro or Enterprise — is billed in rand by card or EFT through PayFast. Receipts for that subscription sit on the billing page. Cancelling keeps access until the end of the period already paid.

10 · Care of the property

From a request to a resolved job.

Maintenance is shared on purpose. The tenant reports it, the landlord owns it, the caretaker can move it, and the contractor does the work.

1

Report

The tenant submits the issue from the portal. Priority can be high, medium, or low.

2

Triage

It lands on the landlord board as open, with a badge in the navigation. AI can draft an assessment of the issue.

3

Assign

The landlord attaches a vendor. A contractor with a portal login then sees it under My Jobs. A caretaker sees it for their properties.

4

Close

The job moves through in progress to resolved. Completion notes and an invoice total stay on the record, and the tenant can see the status change.

Inspections, documents, and the paper trail

Inspections capture the condition of a unit at the moments that matter — moving in, a routine visit, moving out — and caretakers can record them for properties they are assigned to. Documents uploaded by the landlord appear in the tenant’s Documents tab. Notices are posted into the same portal, so a formal message is not trapped in a chat thread.

Communications keeps a log of how the landlord has actually reached someone: WhatsApp, email, phone, SMS, an in-person visit, a notice, or a letter. Automated messages — rent reminders, receipts, overdue notices, lease-expiry notes, maintenance updates, and a welcome — can be logged as they go out, and follow-ups that are due surface on the home screen and on the notification bell.

Leases coming to an end

The Leases page and the dashboard expiration list show who is approaching the end of a term. Renewal is a decision the landlord makes with that date in view, rather than a surprise in the last week. QR codes can be generated for sharing a NestIQ link on paper or on a noticeboard.

Tenants can also leave a review of a property. Those reviews are stored against the property, so a listing can carry a rating that came from someone who lived there.

11 · Everyday tools

The quiet features around the work.

Once the tenancy is running, a set of smaller tools keeps the portfolio understandable: where the buildings are, what the numbers say, and what a careful second opinion would suggest.

Assistance from Gemini

AI features are optional. The landlord adds their own Gemini API key in Settings and can switch individual tools on or off. When a tool runs, the screen says the reply is powered by Gemini. The set covers:

  • Rent suggestions for pricing a unit.
  • Rent-increase advice when a renewal is coming.
  • Tenant screening notes while an application is open.
  • Lease clause drafts to start an agreement.
  • Maintenance assessments when a request arrives.
  • Portfolio analysis across the buildings as a whole.

These are drafts and signals for the landlord to accept, edit, or ignore. They do not approve a tenant or change a rent figure on their own.

Map

Properties plotted so a portfolio spread across a city can be seen geographically, not only as a list.

Calendar

Rent due, overdue amounts, and lease ends on one month, each event tied back to the tenant.

Reports

Rent roll, tax summary, and portfolio reports exported as PDFs the landlord can file or send on.

Language, theme, and reach

The product speaks English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, isiXhosa, and Sesotho. A landlord can restyle the interface — gold, ocean, forest, sunset, midnight, or classic — and switch to dark mode. Notifications collect the events that matter: a payment recorded, a property added, a notice posted, a document generated. Help articles cover getting started, billing, email delivery, and the usual sign-in problems, and the public changelog records what has shipped alongside a roadmap people can vote on.

Email delivery. Automated mail — welcome, trial reminders, rent, leases, and maintenance — is sent through the landlord’s own EmailJS account. Settings stores the public key, the service, and a template for each kind of message, so the wording stays under the landlord’s control.

How the product is held together

NestIQ is a web application. Sign-in, the database, and file storage run on Firebase, with security rules that follow the role: a landlord reads their portfolio, a tenant reads their tenancy, a caretaker reads assigned properties, a contractor reads assigned jobs. Cloud functions handle scheduled email, PayFast subscription events, and submitted reviews. The interface is a single app with a page for each job, so a phone and a desktop share the same account.

Landlord email must be verified before full use. Each role is pinned to its own dashboard, so a stale link cannot drop a tenant into the landlord home. Data is stored with industry-standard encryption. NestIQ does not sell it. Only the landlord and the people they have admitted — tenants, caretakers, contractors — reach the records that belong to them.
